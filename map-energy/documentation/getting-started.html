Getting Started with MAP Energy

Connect your energy system, explore your live energy data and configure the integrations that matter to you. This guide will take you through the main steps to get MAP Energy up and running.

Welcome to MAP Energy

MAP Energy brings your home energy information together in one place, with a particular focus on Tesla Powerwall monitoring, control and automation.

Once connected, you can see how energy is moving around your home, review historical usage, connect supported electricity suppliers and EV chargers, view solar information and build automations around your energy system.

You do not need to configure every integration immediately. The best place to start is by creating your MAP Energy account and connecting Tesla Energy.

✓
Start simple

Connect your Powerwall first and make sure your Live data is working. You can add your supplier, EV charger, diagnostics and automations afterwards.

Your first MAP Energy setup

Follow these steps when setting up MAP Energy for the first time.

1
Install MAP Energy

Install MAP Energy on your supported iPhone, iPad or Android device and open the app.

2
Create your MAP Energy account

Follow the registration process in the app to create your MAP Energy account and sign in.

3
Connect Tesla Energy

Start the Tesla connection from MAP Energy and sign in through Tesla's authentication process. Review the requested permissions and authorise MAP Energy to access your supported Tesla Energy information.

4
Select your energy site

Once Tesla is connected, MAP Energy can load the supported energy sites available to your Tesla account. Select the site you want to use with MAP Energy.

5
Check your Live screen

Confirm that MAP Energy is showing your Powerwall state of charge and available energy flows such as solar, battery, home, grid and EV charging.

6
Add your optional integrations

Once the main Tesla connection is working, you can connect supported electricity suppliers, EV chargers and other MAP Energy features relevant to your setup.

Connecting Tesla Energy

Your Tesla connection is at the centre of most MAP Energy Powerwall functionality.

MAP Energy uses Tesla's account authentication process to connect to supported Tesla Energy systems. You will be asked to sign in to Tesla and approve the permissions required for the features you want to use.

After authentication completes, MAP Energy can associate your selected Tesla Energy site with your MAP Energy account.

i
Tesla may occasionally require you to reconnect

Tesla authentication can expire or require renewed authorisation. If MAP Energy tells you that your Tesla account needs to be reconnected, follow the connection process again from the app.

Review energy history and costs

Use History to compare reported generation, consumption, grid import and export, battery activity and supported EV charging over time. Where tariff and energy data are available, review import costs, export value and net energy cost.

Net energy cost is import cost minus export value. It is not the same as a guaranteed saving or your complete supplier bill. Missing readings, tariff changes and billing adjustments can affect the comparison; check your supplier for the definitive bill.

Connect your electricity supplier

Supplier connections are optional, but they can give MAP Energy additional information about your electricity tariff, prices and supported smart tariff events.

Supported supplier connections include Octopus Energy, EDF and Amber Electric. Octopus and EDF smart tariff features depend on your account: examples include Intelligent Octopus Go, EDF Smart Bolt-On and EDF Power Perks. Amber Electric provides supported tariff and price information; it does not imply the same smart charging events as Octopus or EDF.

If your supplier is supported, open the relevant connection option in MAP Energy and follow the instructions shown in the app.

i
Not every supplier feature is available everywhere

Supplier capabilities can vary by country, supplier, tariff and account type. MAP Energy will only display features that are supported by the relevant integration.

Connect your EV charger

If you have a supported EV charger, connecting it can give MAP Energy a better understanding of when your vehicle is charging and how EV charging fits into your wider home energy use.

Supported integrations include Tesla Wall Connector, myenergi Zappi, Hypervolt and Ohme, with capabilities varying between integrations.

EV charging events can also be used with MAP Energy automations, for example to change Powerwall behaviour when charging starts or stops.

Configure your solar system

If your home has solar PV, MAP Energy can use information about your installation to provide supported solar forecasting features.

Enter the requested details for your solar installation, such as system size and orientation, as accurately as possible. These values help the forecasting service model the expected output from your panels.

Where separate PV array configuration is available, enter each array's capacity, tilt and orientation individually—for example, east-facing and west-facing panels. Use the forecasting connection offered in your app. Forecasts estimate future generation; Live and History show reported generation. Supported solar forecast thresholds can also trigger automations.

Create your first automation

Once you are comfortable with the live information shown by MAP Energy, you can start using automations to make your energy system respond automatically.

An automation combines a trigger with one or more actions. You can also use supported conditions and time windows to control when the automation is allowed to run.

For example, an EV charging event could trigger a change to your Powerwall backup reserve, or a supported smart tariff event could trigger a Powerwall action or Home Assistant webhook.

Start with a simple automation

A notification-only automation is a useful way to understand how triggers behave before using an automation that changes your Powerwall settings.
